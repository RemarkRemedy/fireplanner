Building a FIRE portfolio in Singapore comes down to one core principle: keep costs low and stay diversified. Every 0.1% in fees compounds against you over a 20-30 year accumulation phase.

This guide covers the most cost-effective ETFs accessible to Singapore-based investors, organised by asset class.

## Global Equity ETFs

### Why Ireland-domiciled?

Singapore investors benefit from Ireland-domiciled ETFs due to the Ireland-US tax treaty, which reduces US dividend withholding tax from 30% to 15%. This is a meaningful difference over decades of compounding.

For a US-only ETF like VUAA at 0.07% TER, the lower fee is attractive, but you lose diversification outside the US. Most FIRE planners prefer VWRA or ISAC for their all-in-one global coverage.

A35 (ABF Singapore Bond Index) is the only SGD-denominated bond ETF on this list, which eliminates currency risk for Singapore-based retirees. However, it has a higher TER and lower yield compared to global alternatives.

For accumulation phase (10+ years to FIRE), currency risk on USD-denominated bonds is less concerning since you have time to ride out FX fluctuations.

## Sample FIRE Portfolio Allocations

Here are two common allocations for Singapore FIRE portfolios:

**Aggressive (20+ years to FIRE):**
- 90% VWRA (global equity)
- 10% AGGU (global bonds)

**Moderate (10-15 years to FIRE):**
- 70% VWRA (global equity)
- 20% AGGU (global bonds)
- 10% A35 or CLR (SGD bonds or S-REITs)

The key is keeping total portfolio TER below 0.25%. At $500,000 invested, a 0.25% TER costs $1,250/year. At 1.5% (typical unit trust), that same portfolio costs $7,500/year.

## Where to Buy These ETFs

Singapore investors can access these ETFs through several brokers:

- **Interactive Brokers (IBKR):** Lowest commissions for LSE/SGX-listed ETFs. Best for portfolios above $50,000.
- **Tiger Brokers / moomoo:** Competitive fees for SGX-listed ETFs. Good for smaller portfolios.
- **FSMOne / Endowus:** SRS-compatible platforms for tax-advantaged investing.

For SRS investing specifically, Endowus and FSMOne offer SRS-compatible fund portfolios that include some of these underlying ETFs in fund-of-fund structures.

## Common Mistakes

### Buying US-domiciled ETFs (VT, VOO, BND)

US-domiciled ETFs like VT and VOO have lower TERs but expose Singapore investors to US estate tax (up to 40% on assets above $60,000) and 30% dividend withholding tax. Ireland-domiciled equivalents (VWRA, VUAA) avoid estate tax entirely and reduce withholding to 15%.

### Over-diversifying into too many ETFs

A FIRE portfolio does not need 10 ETFs. One global equity ETF (VWRA) and one bond ETF (AGGU) covers over 4,000 securities across 50+ countries. Adding more ETFs increases rebalancing complexity without meaningful diversification benefit.

### Ignoring the bid-ask spread on SGX

Some SGX-listed ETFs have wide bid-ask spreads due to low trading volume. Check the spread before buying. If it exceeds 0.3%, consider using a limit order or buying the LSE-listed equivalent through IBKR.

## Summary

1. Use Ireland-domiciled ETFs for tax efficiency (VWRA, ISAC, AGGU)
2. Keep total portfolio TER below 0.25%
3. One or two ETFs is sufficient for most FIRE portfolios
4. Use SRS for additional tax savings on your ETF investments
5. Avoid US-domiciled ETFs due to estate tax and higher withholding
