The Supplementary Retirement Scheme (SRS) is one of the most effective tax optimisation tools available in Singapore. Yet many eligible individuals either overlook it or use it sub-optimally.

This guide walks through how to get the most out of your SRS contributions.

## What Is SRS?

SRS is a voluntary savings scheme that provides tax relief on contributions and preferential tax treatment on withdrawals. It sits alongside CPF as a second pillar of retirement savings.

**Key facts:**

- **Contribution cap (Citizens/PRs):** S$15,300 per year
- **Contribution cap (Foreigners):** S$35,700 per year
- **Tax relief:** Dollar-for-dollar deduction from taxable income
- **Withdrawal age:** From 62 (statutory retirement age at time of first contribution, currently 63 for new accounts)
- **Penalty-free withdrawal:** Only 50% of withdrawals are taxable after reaching the statutory retirement age
- **Penalty withdrawal:** 100% taxable plus a 5% penalty if withdrawn before the statutory retirement age

## Step 1: Determine If SRS Is Worth It for You

SRS makes the most sense when your current marginal tax rate is higher than your expected effective tax rate at withdrawal.

**Example:** If you earn S$120,000 annually, your marginal tax rate is 15%. Contributing S$15,300 saves you S$2,295 in taxes this year.

At withdrawal (after 62/63), only 50% of the amount is taxable. If you withdraw S$40,000/year, only S$20,000 is added to your taxable income, which would fall into the 0-2% bracket if SRS is your only income.

**The breakeven point:** If your annual income is below ~S$40,000, the tax savings may not justify locking up the funds until 62/63. The 0% tax bracket covers the first S$20,000 of chargeable income.

## Step 2: Contribute the Full Cap Annually

If SRS makes sense for your income level, contribute the full S$15,300 each year. The contribution window is the calendar year (January to December).

**Timing tip:** You can contribute any time during the year. Some people set up a standing instruction with their SRS operator (DBS, OCBC, or UOB) to auto-contribute monthly or annually.

**Common mistake:** Contributing but not investing. Uninvested SRS cash earns 0.05% interest, which means inflation erodes your savings. Always invest your SRS funds.

## Step 3: Invest Your SRS Funds

SRS funds can be invested in a range of instruments:

- **Unit trusts / funds** (through SRS operator or platforms like Endowus, FSMOne)
- **Singapore Government Securities** (T-bills, SGS bonds)
- **Fixed deposits**
- **Exchange-traded funds (ETFs)** via CPFIS-approved brokers
- **Single stocks** (SGX-listed)
- **Insurance products** (endowment, ILPs)

**For long-term growth (10+ years to withdrawal):**

A diversified global equity fund or ETF portfolio is generally suitable for long-term SRS investing. Platforms like Endowus offer SRS-compatible fund portfolios with low fees.

**For shorter horizons (under 5 years to withdrawal):**

Consider lower-risk options like Singapore Savings Bonds, T-bills, or fixed deposits to preserve capital.

## Step 4: Plan Your Withdrawal Strategy

At the statutory retirement age, you have a **10-year withdrawal window** during which withdrawals receive the 50% tax concession. After 10 years, the account is deemed fully withdrawn and taxed accordingly.

**Optimal withdrawal approach:**

Spread withdrawals evenly across the 10-year window to minimise the taxable amount each year. If your SRS balance is S$400,000 at withdrawal age:

- **10 equal withdrawals:** S$40,000/year, of which S$20,000 is taxable
- S$20,000 taxable income falls within the 0-2% bracket
- Effective tax on each withdrawal: ~S$200 (0.5% effective rate)

Compare this to withdrawing S$400,000 in a lump sum: S$200,000 taxable, resulting in ~S$17,000+ in taxes.

## Step 5: Coordinate with CPF and Other Income

Your withdrawal strategy should account for all retirement income sources:

- **CPF LIFE payouts** (from age 65)
- **Investment income** (dividends, rental)
- **Part-time work** (if applicable)

Since all income is aggregated for tax purposes, plan SRS withdrawals for years when your other income is lowest.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Not investing SRS contributions

Leaving SRS as cash for years is one of the most costly mistakes. At 0.05% interest with 3% inflation, you lose roughly 2.95% of purchasing power annually.

### Mistake 2: Early withdrawal

Withdrawing before the statutory retirement age means 100% of the amount is taxable plus a 5% penalty. Unless you are facing a genuine financial emergency, avoid early withdrawal.

### Mistake 3: Forgetting the 10-year window

The 10-year withdrawal window starts from your first penalty-free withdrawal. If you delay your first withdrawal, you still only get 10 years from that point. Plan ahead.

### Mistake 4: Over-contributing

SRS contributions above the annual cap are not eligible for tax relief and will still be locked in. Double-check your contribution amount each year.

## SRS vs CPF SA Top-Up: Which Is Better?

Both provide tax relief, but they serve different purposes:

- **CPF SA Top-Up (RSTU):** S$8,000 cap, 4% guaranteed return, locked until 55, funds go to CPF LIFE
- **SRS:** S$15,300 cap, returns depend on investment, locked until 62/63, flexible withdrawal over 10 years

If you can afford both, doing both maximises your total tax relief (up to S$23,300/year combined). If you must choose one, CPF SA top-up generally offers better risk-adjusted returns due to the guaranteed 4% rate.

## Summary

1. Check if your income justifies SRS contributions (generally above S$40,000)
2. Contribute the full S$15,300 annually
3. Invest in a diversified portfolio matching your time horizon
4. Plan withdrawals across the 10-year window to minimise taxes
5. Coordinate with CPF LIFE and other income sources
